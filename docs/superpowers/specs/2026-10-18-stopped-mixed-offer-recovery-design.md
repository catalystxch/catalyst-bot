# Stopped campaign mixed terminal offer recovery

Status: design for review. This work does not authorize a live wallet action or a public release.

## Intent and observed case

An operator needs CATalyst to reconcile an elapsed bootstrap campaign whose bot is stopped and whose exact Sage offers are no longer live, even when some offers filled and others expired. The Harvestr original profile is the proving case: one buy has authoritative fill evidence, five other offers have authoritative natural-expiry evidence, Sage has zero live/fillable offers and zero pending transactions, and the durable `REGISTRY_BLOCKED` latch names exactly the six `reconcile:<intent_id>` operations. Its filled root is `unknown` with an `UNKNOWN` journal; the five expiry roots are `visible`. The campaign remains active at revision 0 but its time has elapsed. The original database has not been reconciled.

Success means the native app shows the six exact outcomes before committing; after a confirmed proof-only commit, the existing Task 9 reconciliation records one fill and five expiries exactly once, its DB offer projection matches Sage, and the latch resolves only if every named blocker is independently closed. The bot stays stopped and the campaign is not stopped, renewed, or restarted by this operation. There is no wallet transaction, fee, offer creation, cancellation, marketplace publication, or Splash dispatch.

## Why existing controls cannot serve this case

`Verify Completed Offers` accepts only `created` or `visible` roots and requires every absent offer to be `FILLED_PROVEN`. `Verify Expired Offers` accepts only `created` or `visible` roots and requires every target to be `EXPIRED_PROVEN`. Neither accepts this mixed cohort or its `unknown` fill root. Both normal commit routes also cross the app bridge mutation guard and Flask write hook, which deny a tripped durable latch before any proof is checked. A simple third button calling either route would fail, and broadly disabling the mutation gate would create trading authority that the evidence does not support.

## Scope and authority

The new `Verify Terminal Offers` control is a narrow, explicit proof-only recovery entry in the restricted native view as well as the normal stopped-bot campaign view. It is available only for an **active campaign whose expiry has elapsed**, with an exact campaign ID and revision, a stopped bot, an owned and renewing runtime lease, synced Sage on the campaign's network/fingerprint/wallet ID/asset, and a complete Sage offer/pending snapshot. The safety latch must be either allowed or tripped with the expected `REGISTRY_BLOCKED` reason, expected generation, and no blocker outside the exact campaign's `reconcile:<intent_id>` set. A different latch reason, changed owner, extra blocker, another active campaign, unresolved creation, pending transaction, or any live Sage offer denies commit.

The restricted entry may read Sage and write only Task 9 terminal evidence, its exact offer/coin projections, durable pending post-fill hook records, and the existing reconciliation journal/latch transitions. It must not acquire a generic mutation permit, invoke wallet mutation, deliver post-fill hooks, grant bot/Coin Prep/start/publication authority, or make a general exception for other API or bridge calls. Local-origin, local-token, rate limits, request validation, and native bridge callable restrictions still apply. A direct HTTP request and a direct bridge call must face the same proof rules; the route cannot be made usable merely by skipping those guards without a replacement scoped fence.

Because normal lease takeover rejects a tripped latch, a separate registry-terminal restricted startup path must prove the exact elapsed campaign, `REGISTRY_BLOCKED` generation and reconcile-only blocker set, configured Sage binding, two fresh identity and complete book snapshots, and absence of pending/live offers before a lease compare-and-set. It may adopt an expired lease only after proving its prior process dead; an active owner or uncertain liveness denies entry. The database adoption binds the new run ID/PID/host, lease acquisition epoch, wallet hash/network/asset, campaign ID/revision, latch generation/blockers, and observed book. After acquisition the app repeats independent proof naming the new owner, starts only the lease heartbeat and restricted native view, and keeps normal startup, autostart and all general mutation routes closed. Failure releases only the newly owned lease and retains the latch. A crash or restart repeats the same proof; it never treats a stale lease row as live authority.

## Review and commit contract

1. Preview validates the exact campaign and runtime authority, waits for offer creation quiescence, and collects its complete campaign-root set: unresolved intents with matching open offer projections plus roots already terminalized by a partial prior attempt. It rejects duplicate/missing trade IDs, non-bootstrap purpose, wrong revision, parent/child lineage, a mismatched asset/side/tier, or more than 256 targets. For the proving case it must account for all six roots, including the `unknown` filled root; an `unknown` root is admissible only when its exact existing reconciliation journal and blocker match the selected intent. An already-terminal root needs its exact durable Task 9 proof and journal, and is displayed but never reapplied. A root with an unknown wallet effect but no conclusive fill proof is denied.
2. Preview takes two complete Sage book and pending snapshots around authoritative evidence collection. It requires stable identity, campaign revision, intent rows, offer projection, lease, latch generation/blocker set, and Sage book. For every unresolved absent trade, `classify_terminal_evidence` must return exactly `FILLED_PROVEN` or `EXPIRED_PROVEN`. The preview returns a sorted per-intent vector of intent ID, trade ID, side, tier, classification, and a digest of canonical immutable proof fields and authority bindings; observation timestamps alone do not change the digest. It reports zero wallet effects. It does not mutate the DB.
3. Commit receives only the exact reviewed campaign/revision, latch generation, and per-intent vector/digest. Under the campaign and bot lifecycle locks it repeats all preview checks with fresh Sage evidence and compare-and-set checks of identity, lease, campaign, target rows, journals, latch, and snapshot. Lease stability means the same run ID/PID/host and acquisition epoch with a current owned, unexpired heartbeat; the heartbeat's incrementing lease version is read and fenced at the final compare-and-set, not required to stay unchanged across preview time. Any changed owner/epoch, classification, missing root, new pending/live offer, new blocker, or stale digest refuses the whole next step. It never trusts the browser's claimed outcome.
4. Commit reconciles `FILLED_PROVEN` roots before `EXPIRED_PROVEN` roots through the existing Task 9 reconciliation function with an explicit recovery-only hook deferral. The fill proof must close the matching `UNKNOWN` journal and record its financial/coin result once; an expiry must close only its own visible root. Every applied result must match the prechecked class and exact intent ID. Existing `reconcile_offer()` calls `_run_post_fill_hooks()` after a fill; the recovery path must not do so. It leaves the fill's durable hook outbox pending, with no claims, callbacks, BoostManager, SweepCoordinator, notification or Splash delivery. The response reports pending hook count separately from terminal reconciliation. No campaign status, budget, or fee approval is altered.
5. A DB failure after some terminal records leaves the durable latch tripped. The response says a partial commit may have occurred and requires a fresh preview; it does not claim a completed recovery. Retry discovers already-terminal records, checks their durable evidence and journal, and only applies still-open exact roots. It never replays a fill as a second fill, invents an expiry, or widens the original target set. The latch may resolve only by the existing blocker resolver after all six exact operations are proven closed and no unrelated blocker exists. The pending hook outbox is not silently discarded or treated as delivered. A separate, explicit normal-authority drain must be implemented and verified before public acceptance; it cannot run through this restricted recovery permit, and its own effects and exactly-once acknowledgements need independent review.

## Operator presentation

The native restricted screen names the campaign, wallet identity, elapsed status, latch reason/generation, and each proposed outcome. The button is disabled until preview proves every target. The final confirmation describes a database-only proof reconciliation and states that trading will stay stopped. After commit, show counts for filled and expired, any partial-failure state, remaining blockers, Sage live/pending counts, and campaign/bot status. Do not suggest a fill was witnessed by Splash or Dexie; Sage and chain proof are the authority. A normal dashboard must not silently appear merely because a preview succeeded.

## Refusals and verification

Use disposable databases and mock Sage for red/green tests covering the exact one-fill/five-expiry case; `unknown` with its matching journal; an unsupported unknown root; altered proof between preview and commit; changed wallet/lease owner or acquisition epoch, lease heartbeat version, latch generation or blockers; active live offer; pending transaction; wrong campaign/revision; unresolved creation; duplicate or unrelated intents; partial commit/crash/retry; exact fill accounting once; and unchanged campaign/fee ledger. Test stale-owner adoption, active/uncertain-owner refusal, loss of the new owner, HTTP, direct bridge, restricted native UI, and normal mutation controls under a tripped latch. Real post-fill hook integration tests must prove recovery leaves each hook pending without invoking its callback, then a separate normal-authority drain delivers or durably refuses each effect exactly once; monkeypatching `_run_post_fill_hooks()` away is insufficient. Assert no wallet mutation, fee submission, offer creation, publication, notification, BoostManager, SweepCoordinator or Splash dispatch on every restricted recovery path. The existing all-filled and all-expired controls must retain their current behavior.

Run targeted and full backend/frontend suites, package and Defender checks, independent review, then isolated packaged smoke. Before any original-profile launch or commit, recheck exact source/hash/PID, Sage identity/balances/full offers/pending, campaign, fee ledger, lease/latch, and GUI. First prove the same mixed cohort on an online disposable copy; then use the native reviewed path on Harvestr with action-time Windows confirmation. Keep the PR draft and keep public-readiness blocked until original-profile end state and the other release gates are verified.
